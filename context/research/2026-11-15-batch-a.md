Terms:

- A **landmark** is a region a screen reader can list and jump to, such as `nav` or `search`.
- **Roving focus** gives a set of controls one Tab stop; the arrow keys move between them.
- A **live region** is an element whose text changes are spoken without focus moving there. Kuiper's is `status`.
- A **focus move** is focus going somewhere the person did not Tab or arrow to (`022` decision 11).

## In short

- **The six are cheap, but three of them speak or move focus.** Pagination (in its button form), the search field and the tag move focus or announce. They join `022`'s screen-reader set. Link, button group and progress bar have no live region, focus move or overlay (INFERRED, `022` decision 11's signals).
- **bits-ui helps less than the inventory thought.** Its Pagination renders a `div` with buttons, sets no `aria-current` and no landmark, and adds arrow keys on top of Tab stops. Its Progress is a `div` with `role="progressbar"`, whose fill needs a `style` width that Kuiper's literal lint forbids. bits-ui has no Link (EXECUTED, `node_modules/bits-ui` 2.20.2, below).
- **The native elements do the work.** A `<progress>` has the right role and takes its value as an attribute. An `<input type="search">` is a `searchbox`. An `<a href>` is a link and is SvelteKit's router link (READ, ARIA in HTML).
- **Breadth.** Five of the six are parts the scorecard counts and Kuiper lacks: pagination, search, button group, tag, link. "Progress / meter" is already counted, through `meter`. So the batch moves row 1 from 30 (with toast) to 35 of 40 (EXECUTED, `scripts/bench/targets.ts` lines 209–286).

## 1. Kuiper's bases

### What Kuiper already has (EXECUTED, read)

- **`button`** has a `link` variant (`text-primary underline underline-offset-4`, no side padding) and renders an `<a>` when given `href`. A disabled `href` button becomes `role="link"`, `aria-disabled`, `tabindex="-1"` (`src/lib/registry/ui/button/button.svelte`).
- **`input-group`** wraps an `input` with addons and one focus ring. Its `InputGroupPassword` is a worked example of a control with a built-in button: the button's `aria-controls` takes the input's id from `id`, then the `pluto:field` context, then `$props.id()` (`input-group-password.svelte`). Clicking an addon focuses the input (`input-group-addon.svelte`).
- **`badge`** is a static `span`, mono text, outlined in its tone. Its default border is `border-border`, the 10% hairline (`badge.svelte`).
- **`meter`** is a native `<meter>` styled through `pluto-theme`'s rules on `[data-slot="meter"]` and its pseudo-elements (`src/app.css` lines 854–875). `004` decision 5 put them in the theme because "pseudo-elements cannot be classes" (READ, `specifications/004-code-and-data.md` line 183).
- **`status`** is an empty polite live region, present from the first render. Setting the same text twice is not spoken again (`status.svelte`).
- **Inline links** are per-item classes today: `underline underline-offset-4`, often `min-h-6` (24 px) on standalone links, and `hover:text-foreground` in muted text. Ten items repeat them (EXECUTED, `grep -rn underline src/lib/registry`).

### What the lint and the tokens allow (EXECUTED, read)

- **No `style` attribute** in an item (`scripts/lint-literals.ts`, header). So a fill drawn by `style="width: 40%"` or a `transform` is out.
- **Arbitrary variants are classes.** The lint splits a class into variants and utility (`splitClass`, line 401) and judges the utility. Items already use `[&>a]:underline`. So `[&::-webkit-progress-value]:bg-primary` should pass: its utility is a token colour (INFERRED; `027`'s first test proves it).
- **Animations** are limited to `animate-enter`, `animate-exit` and `animate-spin` (L10, line 495). An indeterminate bar's sweep needs a keyframe, which would be a theme change (lane T).
- **Named colours fail** the lint, so `bg-[Highlight]` is out. A system-colour allowance is `028`'s to add.
- **Tokens that fit:** `--size-track` (6 px), `meter`'s 8 px height, `--focus-width` (2 px), `--primary`, `--muted`, `--destructive`, `--input` (the solid control edge, `#857e70` in light), `--card`, `--accent`.
- **`--border` is translucent** (`#0b0f171a`). `019a` decision 3 keeps alpha for layers above content, and `031b` will lint it. A new item should take `border-input`.

### bits-ui, as pinned (EXECUTED, `node_modules/bits-ui/dist/bits/`, `bun.lock` line 347: `bits-ui@2.20.2`)

- **Pagination** (`pagination/`):
  - The root is a `div` with no role and no label (`components/pagination.svelte`).
  - Pages are `<button type="button">` with `aria-label="Page N"` and `data-selected`. There is no `aria-current` (`pagination.svelte.js`, `PaginationPageState.props`).
  - Prev and next take native `disabled` at the ends.
  - The arrow keys, Home and End move focus across prev, pages and next. Every item stays a Tab stop, so it is not roving focus (`handleTriggerKeydown`).
  - `getPageItems` always shows the first and last page, siblings either side, and ellipses at the gaps. Its comment credits Melt UI.
  - There is no link mode. A `child` snippet could render an `<a>`, but the button's `disabled` and its Space handler would follow it.
- **Progress** (`progress/`): a `div` with `role="progressbar"`, `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax` and `aria-valuenow` (omitted when `value` is `null`), and `data-state` of `indeterminate`, `loading` or `loaded`. It draws nothing. It also sets a `value` attribute on the `div`.
- **Button** (`button/`): an `a` with `href` or a `button`. A disabled link becomes `role="link"`, `aria-disabled`, `tabindex="-1"`. This is what Kuiper's `button` already does.
- **No Link.** `link-preview` is a hover card.
- **Escape in a modal.** bits-ui's escape layer listens for `keydown` on `document`, in the bubbling phase, and calls `preventDefault()` (`utilities/escape-layer/use-escape-layer.svelte.js` lines 28–39). So an input that stops an Escape's propagation keeps a dialog open.

### shadcn-svelte's stock items (READ, sv `pagination.json`, `button-group.json`, `progress.json`)

- **Pagination** wraps bits-ui. It gives the root `role="navigation"` with a lowercase `aria-label="pagination"`. Prev and next are named "Go to previous page" and "Go to next page", and their words are hidden below `sm`. The ellipsis is `aria-hidden`, with a visually hidden "More pages" inside it that is never spoken.
- **Button group** is `<div role="group">`. Horizontal drops the inner radius and the start border of every child after the first. A focused child gets `z-10`. It has `ButtonGroupSeparator` and `ButtonGroupText`. Its docs say: Tab between the buttons, and name the group.
- **Progress** wraps bits-ui. The fill is a `div` moved by an inline `transform: translateX(…)` style.

## 2. Pagination

### Semantics and pattern

- The WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices have no pagination pattern. The references converge on a `nav` landmark, named, holding a list, with the current page marked `aria-current="page"` (READ, below).
- A page that has a URL should be a link (Primer: "Every page should have a URL", `primer-design/content/components/pagination.mdx` line 26). A table paged in place, with no URL, uses buttons (Carbon `PaginationNav`).

GOV.UK's guidance (GD `components/pagination/index.md`, paraphrased) also says to put the page number in the document's title, so a screen-reader user knows the page changed. It says to omit "previous" on the first page and "next" on the last.

### Kuiper should (INFERRED)

- **A `nav`, named "Pagination"**, its name replaceable. Three parts, as GOV.UK lays them out: previous, the list of pages, next. Prev and next sit outside the list, so the list counts pages only.
- **Links when the app gives an `href` builder; buttons when it does not.** Both modes keep one shape.
- **The current page** carries `aria-current="page"`. Its visible sign is a border and bold weight, so the state is shape, not colour.
- **Names.** Each page's visible number has a visually hidden "Page " before it, so its name is "Page 3", with no `aria-label` to override the visible text (SC 2.5.3 holds). Prev and next show "Previous" and "Next" with a hidden " page".
- **The ends.** Omit prev on the first page and next on the last (GOV.UK). Keep the slot, so the row does not jump. In button mode, if the pressed control vanishes, move focus to the new current page. Disabled controls are not served: there is nothing to do there.
- **The ellipsis** is hidden from assistive technology. The page numbers either side already tell the gap. This departs from GOV.UK, which exposes "⋯"; Kuiper's list then counts only what can be pressed.
- **Tab only.** No arrow keys: links in a `nav` are Tab stops in every reference but bits-ui.
- **Narrow.** Below a container width, hide the page list and show "Page 3 of 12" as text. Prev and next stay. A container query does it in CSS.
- **In button mode, say the new page.** A polite status, "Page 4 of 12", as Carbon's PaginationNav does. Focus stays on the pressed control, so nothing else tells a screen-reader user that the content changed. In link mode the new page's title says it; that is the app's part.
- **Nothing with one page or none.**
- **Not bits-ui.** Its root, its missing `aria-current`, its button-only pages and its extra keys would each need overriding. Kuiper writes its own page-range function (pure, unit-tested) and draws pages with `button`'s `buttonVariants`.

## 3. Search field

### Semantics and pattern

- `<input type="search">` has the implicit role `searchbox` (READ, ARIA in HTML, W3C Recommendation, 11 August 2026, `w3.org/TR/html-aria/`).
- The `search` element has the implicit role `search`, a landmark. It is in every engine since 2023 (READ, the same; its row was added on 24 March 2023).
- A landmark belongs to a page's search region, not to every filter box.

### What each reference does

| | Element | Landmark | Label | Clear button | Escape | Announces results |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Carbon** `Search` (CR `Search/Search.tsx`) | `type="search"` (:161) | the wrapper `div role="search"`, named by the label (:309) | required, always visually hidden (:77; `_search.scss` :54–55) | `<button>` "Clear search input" (:142, :338–346). Hidden while empty, a Tab stop when filled (CW `search/accessibility.mdx` :45–47). Focus returns to the input (:234) | clears when filled, with `stopPropagation` (:242–246) | no; flagged as a designer's note (CW :131–140) |
| **Spectrum 2** (SD `search-field.json`) | role `textbox` (:113–116) | not stated | "Search" by default, inside the field (:51); an icon must be present if there is no label | in-field; Tab reaches it; clearing closes a results popover (:35, :63) | not stated | not stated; "No results" instead of an error state (:39) |
| **Primer** (docs, `text-input.mdx`) | a text input with a leading visual | not stated | visible required; hidden only when equivalent visible text exists (:154–164) | a trailing action, usually clear (:68, :118–120) | not stated | must announce its effect (:167); `ui-patterns/loading.mdx` :274–282: say "5 items match the filter" in an always-rendered `role="status"` |

Carbon and Radix both hide WebKit's own cancel button (`::-webkit-search-cancel-button`), since a drawn clear button replaces it (READ, above).

### Kuiper should (INFERRED)

- **`<input type="search">` inside `input-group`,** with a magnifier icon at the start, `aria-hidden`. The icon is not a label.
- **Named by a label.** A `Label for`, a `Field`, or an `aria-label` when visible text beside it already says what is searched (Primer's rule). The placeholder never names it.
- **No landmark of its own.** A site search is wrapped by the app in `<search>`; a filter box is not. The page shows both.
- **A clear button, shown only while there is text,** named "Clear search". It is a Tab stop while shown (Carbon, Spectrum). Pressing it empties the field, fires `oninput`'s equivalent, and returns focus to the input.
- **Escape clears a filled field** and stops there: `preventDefault` and `stopPropagation`, so a dialog around it stays open. Escape on an empty field does nothing, so the dialog closes. Chromium and WebKit already clear `type="search"` on Escape; Firefox does not. Kuiper's handler makes all three agree.
- **WebKit's cancel button and decoration hidden.**
- **An optional result count, spoken.** The app passes `results` ("12 results"). The field writes it into an always-present `Status` once typing pauses. It writes it again after each pause, so an unchanged count is still heard. This is Primer's rule, built in.

### Kuiper should (INFERRED)

- **`<div role="group">`, named** by `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`. A name makes the grouping heard; without one it is visual only, as Primer warns.
- **Tab to each button.** Roving focus is a toolbar's, and the toolbar is batch C (`042`). Spectrum's arrow-key note contradicts its own `group` role, so it is not followed.
- **Two looks.** *Attached*: joined edges, inner corners square, one shared border between neighbours, for a few related actions of equal weight (Primer, shadcn, Carbon). *Spaced*: a row with a gap that wraps, and stacks below `sm`, for a form's actions (GOV.UK, Spectrum).
- **Attached groups hold secondary buttons.** A primary's border is its fill, so joined primaries show no divider. One primary at most, in a spaced group (Carbon, Primer).
- **Contents:** `Button`s, icon buttons with names, and a `DropdownMenu` trigger for "more" (a split action). Not inputs: `input-group` serves those.
- **No context.** The group styles its children by selector, so `button` does not change (lane I is `023`'s).

### Kuiper should (INFERRED)

- **A tag is a badge you can act on.** A static label stays `badge`. A tag is removable or pressable, never both (Spectrum's one behaviour).
- **A removable tag** is text plus a separate remove `<button>`, named "Remove <label>" (Primer's rule; Carbon's "Dismiss" fails it).
- **A pressable tag** is one `<button aria-pressed>`, with a check icon while pressed, so the state is a shape (Carbon's selectable tag).
- **A tag group** is a named `ul`. Each tag's control is a Tab stop. Arrow-key focus (Spectrum) is left to batch B's multi-select, whose field holds the tags.
- **Delete and Backspace** on a remove button also remove (Primer, Spectrum).
- **Focus after removal:** the next tag's control; else the previous; else the group's list, which takes focus and speaks its name, or a fallback the app names (Primer's "the input"). Next before previous keeps the reading order going (INFERRED; Primer chooses previous, which Q7 of `027` offers).
- **No truncation.** Tags hold short labels. Truncation needs a tooltip on focus (Carbon), and a tooltip does not reach touch.

### Kuiper should (INFERRED)

- **An `<a href>`, always.** In SvelteKit that is the router link; nothing like `asChild` is needed. An action that looks like a link is a `Button`, and the page says so.
- **Always underlined.** SC 1.4.1: colour is not the only sign. Thicker on hover, as GOV.UK does. The global `:focus-visible` outline shows focus.
- **Two colours:** `default` in `primary`, and `inherit` for a link inside muted text or a footer, where the ten items' per-item classes take the current colour today.
- **Standalone:** a link alone on its line, such as in a list, gets a 24 px target (`min-h-6`), as `error-summary` and the footers already do (SC 2.5.8 exempts links inside a sentence).
- **Visited, opt-in,** in `foreground`: no new token. Off by default, as Carbon; GOV.UK's on-by-default suits a site of documents more than an app.
- **A new tab, opt-in,** with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, an `aria-hidden` icon, and a visually hidden "(opens in a new tab)". GOV.UK puts the words in view; Primer says don't do it at all. Kuiper serves it because apps do it, and makes it honest (Q4 of `027`).
- **No disabled link.** A link that goes nowhere is text. Carbon's disabled link is a focusable stop that does nothing.
- **`linkVariants` exported,** so prose rendered from Markdown can take the same classes.

## 7. Progress bar

### Semantics and pattern

- `<progress>` has the implicit role `progressbar`. Authors should not set `aria-valuemax` on it; `aria-*` attributes applicable to `progressbar` are allowed, so `aria-valuetext` is (READ, ARIA in HTML, the `progress` row).
- `<progress>` is a labelable element, so `<label for>` names it (READ, HTML: "labelable elements"; INFERRED that shadcn's example `<Field.Label for>` on a `div role="progressbar"` names nothing, as the subagent read it at sv `examples/progress-label.svelte` :7–11).
- A progress bar is not a live region. Its changes are not spoken unless focus is on it; NVDA can beep for one (INFERRED from reader settings; not run).

### What each reference does

| | Element | Name | Value text | Indeterminate | States | Live | Forced colours |

No reference read has a reduced-motion rule for its indeterminate sweep. Only Carbon handles forced colours (READ, above).

### Kuiper should (INFERRED)

- **A native `<progress>`,** as `meter` is a native `<meter>`. The role, the value and the range come from attributes. No `style` is needed, which the lint forbids.
- **The same API as `meter`:** `value`, `max`, `valuetext`. The app names it with a `Label for` or `aria-label`, and writes the numbers beside it. The fill is never the only sign.
- **Styled by the item's own classes,** through arbitrary pseudo-element variants (`::-webkit-progress-bar`, `::-webkit-progress-value`, `::-moz-progress-bar`), not by the theme. A theme edit is lane T, where `021` runs in wave 1. If the classes do not render in an engine, the rules move to the theme in a small change after `021`.
- **One state beyond the default: `destructive`,** for a job that stopped, beside text that says so. Done is a full bar and the word "Done".
- **No indeterminate form now.** It needs a keyframe, a theme change and a reduced-motion rule. `spinner` serves an unknown wait today. `039` (motion tokens) is the place for it (Q2 of `027`).
- **Silent by design.** The app announces the start, milestones it chooses, and the end, through `status`. The page shows the pattern. So `progress` is not in `022`'s screen-reader set: it has no live region, focus move or overlay. Its role and value are checked in the DOM.
- **Forced colours:** with `appearance: none`, the forced palette would paint the fill as the track and the bar would vanish (INFERRED from CSS Color Adjust; `028` checks it). `forced-color-adjust: none` keeps it drawn until `028` gives it system colours.

## 9. What `025` would measure

- **Main interactions** (`025` decision 9): pagination's Next in button mode; the search field's Escape on a filled field; a tag's Remove. Link, button group and progress have none: a link's work is the browser's, a group's buttons are `button`'s, and a progress bar does not respond to input.
- **Sizes, alone** (INFERRED, from `025`'s figures): `link`, `button-group` and `progress` near the floor plus `cn`, about 11 kB of JS. `pagination` near `button`'s 20.9 kB. `search-field` above that, with `input-group`, `input`, `status` and two icons: about 23 to 26 kB. `tag` with `button` and two icons: about 22 to 24 kB.

## Corrections, 2026-11-15, after `027`'s specification review

The specification review ([`../reviews/027-batch-a-spec-2026-11-15.md`](../reviews/027-batch-a-spec-2026-11-15.md)) ran what this memo had inferred. Its findings correct this memo as follows. The text above stands as first written. Where it disagrees with this section, this section holds.

- **§3, Escape on `type="search"`: the engines were stated backwards.** Chromium and Firefox clear a search input on Escape. WebKit, as Playwright runs it, does not. A `keydown` listener that calls `preventDefault()` stops the native clear in all three (EXECUTED (review)). So Kuiper's handler prevents the native clear and clears the field itself.
- **§1, Escape and bits-ui: why `stopPropagation()` works.** Svelte delegates `keydown`. bits-ui's document listener goes through Svelte's `on()`, whose wrapper runs delegated handlers first and then skips its own handler once `event.cancelBubble` is set. bits-ui has no other Escape handler (READ (review), `svelte` 5.57.3, `src/internal/client/dom/elements/events.js`; `bits-ui` `dist/`). The handler must stay a plain `onkeydown`, not a capture listener.
- **§3, the count Primer means.** Primer ties the announcement to the results filtering returned (`ui-patterns/loading.mdx` line 280), not to a pause in typing. A count read at the pause can belong to the previous query when results come from a server. Kuiper speaks at the later of the pause and the count's arrival, and drops a count set before the last keystroke (`027` decision 4).
- **§3, WebKit's cancel button.** `getComputedStyle(input, "::-webkit-search-cancel-button").appearance` returns `"auto"` in Chromium and `""` in Firefox and WebKit, with or without the class. The class does change the input's end in Chromium and WebKit, pixel for pixel like `-webkit-appearance: none` (EXECUTED (review)). Prove it by screenshot.
- **§2, a container in a row.** A `nav` with `container-type: inline-size` is 0 px wide as a flex item beside other content, in all three engines (EXECUTED (review)). Pagination's `nav` takes `w-full min-w-0`, and in a row the app gives it the remaining width.
- **§2, a pressed page that moves.** Moving a focused element (`insertBefore` on its `li`) drops focus to `body` in all three engines (EXECUTED (review)). A keyed `each` may move nodes, so the pressed page's focus is restored by page number after the update.
- **§2, the narrow form at the end.** While the page list is hidden, the new current page cannot take focus. Carbon's data-table pager moves focus to the other end control when one becomes unavailable (READ (review), `Pagination/Pagination.tsx` lines 464–469); Kuiper does the same.
- **§2, the switch width.** At the default size, seven 48 px pages, two worded end controls and their gaps need about 580 to 600 px, wider than `@md` (INFERRED (review)). The switch is set by size and siblings (`027` decision 3).
- **§5, the tag index.** A tag's place among the list's `li`s is not its control's place among remove buttons when a group mixes kinds. Count among tag controls only.
- **§5, list semantics.** WebKit drops list semantics from a `ul` styled `list-style: none` outside a `nav` (INFERRED (review), WebKit's known heuristic; not run). The tag list takes an explicit `role="list"`.
- **§6, visited links.** `getComputedStyle` reports a visited link's colour as its unvisited one in all three engines, so a script cannot read history (EXECUTED (review)). A test asserts the compiled `:visited` rule instead.
- **§6, `decoration-2`.** This is Tailwind's 2 px step, a size the literal lint does not judge, like `underline-offset-4` in `button`. It is recorded, not tokenised.
- **§7, the progress classes, run.** Compiled with the pinned Tailwind 4.3.3 and Lightning CSS 1.33.0 and drawn by Playwright 1.64.1, the classes draw in all three engines: `--primary` at 10%, `--muted` at 90%, 8 px tall, and `--destructive` for that variant. The literal lint reports 0 violations on every class `027` names (EXECUTED (review)). §1's "should pass" and §10's first open point are settled.
- **§7, forced colours.** With `forced-color-adjust: none` the bar keeps its colours in Chromium and Firefox. Without it, Chromium paints it white at both ends and Firefox repaints it. Playwright does not emulate forced colours in WebKit, so nothing changes there (EXECUTED (review)). Forced-colour checks run in Chromium and Firefox.
- **§7, `null`.** `value` is required by type, but untyped code can pass `null`, which on a `<progress>` would show the engine's own indeterminate animation. Kuiper renders `value ?? 0`, clamped.
- **§9, cost estimates.** `025` now counts `cn` in its floor, so no item's alone figure holds it. Read §9's figures less about 11 kB: `link`, `button-group` and `progress` are about 0.5 to 2 kB of JS. `025`'s `says` must be one of its verbs ("selects" and "updates" here). Its counts start from 35 entries with `026`.
- **§6, aliases.** Carbon uses "progress indicator" for a stepper, so it is no alias for `progress`.

## Further corrections, 2026-11-15, after the re-review

The re-review (the same file, "Re-review, 2026-11-15") added these. Where they disagree with anything above, they hold.

- **The search count without `resultsFor`.** An unchanged count is no change. A `$derived` count over a fixed list re-ran its `$effect` when the count string changed and did not re-run when it stayed the same (EXECUTED (re-review), Svelte 5.57.3). So the field cannot tell "the app answered with the same count" from "the app has not answered". Without `resultsFor`, only `undefined` can mean pending. With `resultsFor`, equality with `value` decides.
- **Focus on click in WebKit.** WebKit does not focus a button on click, as macOS does not: in 11 of 11 clicks the pressed page was not `document.activeElement` (EXECUTED (re-review)). A focus restore after a press acts only when focus was already inside the control's group.
- **Keyed moves.** Svelte 5.57.3 kept a pressed, keyed page node focused across 11 range changes pressed by Enter, in all three engines (EXECUTED (re-review)). The restore is a guard.
- **Pagination's row widths.** Measured in Geist, with `gap-2` across the row and `gap-1` in the list, the widest full rows are 488, 592 and 696 px at the default size for 0, 1 and 2 siblings, and 384, 456 and 528 px at `sm`. The narrow row is 267 px and 238 px (EXECUTED (re-review)).
- **The compiled `:visited` rule** sits inside `@layer utilities`, so a test must walk nested rules to find it (EXECUTED (re-review)).
- **Composition in Safari.** WebKit may fire `compositionend` before the `keydown` that ends a composition, so that `keydown` can carry `isComposing: false` with `keyCode` 229. This is the known behaviour for Enter, INFERRED for Escape, and not run. Guard with both.
- **Read-only fields.** Native Escape leaves a read-only search field's value in all three engines (EXECUTED (re-review)).
