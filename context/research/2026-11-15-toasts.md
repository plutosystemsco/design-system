# Toasts: what the references do, what the rules demand, and the toast Kuiper should build

- **Status:** complete
- **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Author:** a researcher, at the design lead's request
- **Question:** The owner ruled toasts in (`../decisions.md`, "Toasts, the page standard, and questioning the old rules", 2026-11-15). What is the best toast for Kuiper: its semantics, its rules, its API, and the tests that prove it?
- **What it blocks:** the specification that adds `toast`. It supersedes the toast half of the overlays memo, [`2026-11-01-the-overlay-items-…-and-the-toast-question.md`](./2026-11-01-the-overlay-items-dropdown-menu-sheet-dialog-breadcrumb-and-the-toast-question.md) §4, on which `005` decision 14 stood.

## How to read this memo

- **READ:** a file or page says so. Clones are under `context/prior_art/` at the commits in its `README.md`; web pages were read on 2026-11-15.
- **EXECUTED:** we ran a read-only command and saw the result. Nothing in the tree changed but this file.
- **INFERRED:** our reasoning.

**Terms of art.** A **toast** is a short message in a fixed corner, outside the page's flow, that may leave on its own. A **live region** is an element whose changes a screen reader speaks without focus moving there. **Polite** waits for a pause; **assertive** interrupts. An **announcer** is a visually hidden live region used only to speak. A **landmark** is a region a screen reader can list and jump to. The **top layer** is the browser's layer above every `z-index`, which `popover` elements enter.

## Summary

1. **The references agree on most of the contract.** Errors, warnings, actions and loading never time out. Timers pause on hover and focus. A hotkey or landmark reaches the region. Nobody moves focus into a plain toast (READ, §1).
2. **They split on how a toast is spoken.** Sonner and Melt put `aria-live` on each toast. Radix speaks through a separate hidden announcer. React Aria makes each toast a non-modal `alertdialog` holding an `alert`. The separate announcer avoids nested regions and spoken button names (INFERRED, §2).
3. **`svelte-sonner` 1.2.3 still has the faults `005` named.** Every toast is a tab stop, its live regions nest, its timer defaults to 4 s. It now has no runtime dependencies (EXECUTED, §1.6). The faults are in its markup, not its options (INFERRED, §3).
4. **Recommended: option (b), Kuiper's own `toast`, built on `status`.** About 400 lines, no dependency, every rule in code (§4).
5. **One owner question:** do success and info toasts time out (§5)?

## 1. The references

### 1.1 Carbon (READ, Apache-2.0)

- **Timing.** Toasts "persist by default" and may time out after five seconds, so their content should exist elsewhere (`carbon-design-system-carbon-website/src/pages/components/notification/usage.mdx:342-348`). In code `timeout = 0`: nothing times out unless asked (`carbon-design-system-carbon/packages/react/src/components/Notification/Notification.tsx:410`, `:446-459`).
- **Role** defaults to `status`; `alert` and `log` are allowed (`Notification.tsx:372-375`). An actionable notification is an `alertdialog` that traps focus (`:856-858`) and stays until dismissed (`usage.mdx:393-398`).
- **Placement:** top right, newest at the top (`usage.mdx:322-325`).
- **For a region with actions,** it names two approaches: a persistent area, or a `region` reached by a hotkey that returns focus once the user leaves (`accessibility.mdx:101-117`).

### 1.2 Material 3, Spectrum 2 and Atlassian (READ)

- **Material.** The guidelines page returned only its title to our fetcher, so the lab component is cited. `labs/gb/components/snackbar/snackbar-element.ts`: `timeoutMs = 5000`, `0` disables it (line 119); hover and focus pause it with time kept (lines 156-158, about 326-370); the surface is `role="status" aria-live="polite" aria-atomic="true"` around label and actions (about line 418); reduced motion skips its animations (about line 287). INFERRED: its action's label can be spoken with the message.
- **Spectrum 2.** "For accessibility, toasts have a minimum timeout of 5 seconds, and actionable toasts will not auto dismiss." "Timers will pause when the user focuses or hovers over a toast." (S2 `Toast.md`, `reactspectrum.blob.core.windows.net/…/s2-docs/s2/Toast.md`.) React Aria's region is a landmark reached with F6; when a focused toast closes, focus goes to the next toast, else back (`https://react-aria.adobe.com/Toast`). In source (`adobe/react-spectrum` at `360e351`, `packages/react-aria/src/toast/useToast.ts`) a toast is `role: 'alertdialog'`, `'aria-modal': 'false'`, `tabIndex: 0`. Its content is `role: 'alert'`, `aria-hidden` until a layout effect, "required for NVDA announcements". `maxVisibleToasts` defaults to 1.
- **Atlassian flags** (`https://atlassian.design/components/flag/usage`): bottom left, above modals, newest on top. Success, loading, warning and error flags persist. Never auto-dismiss "critical warnings or errors". At most two links. Never mix dismissible and non-dismissible flags in a stack.

### 1.3 Primer and GOV.UK: why neither has one (READ)

- **Primer** moved Toast to `primer-design/content/deprecated-components/toast.mdx`: "Usage for this component is not encouraged". Its notification messaging puts banners at the top of the content, inline messages beside the action, and a dialog's feedback inside the dialog (`content/ui-patterns/notification-messaging.mdx:159-196`). Roselli, 2025-11-10: "GitHub no longer uses toasts because of their accessibility and usability issues" (`https://adrianroselli.com/2020/01/defining-toast-messages.html`).
- **GOV.UK** has a notification banner (`alphagov-govuk-design-system/src/components/notification-banner/index.md`, Crown copyright, described not quoted). It tells the outcome of an action on the next page, before the `h1`, one per page, with `role="alert"` and focus on load (`alphagov-govuk-frontend/…/notification-banner/template.njk:11-18`, `notification-banner.mjs:19-33`). It goes on the page after.
- INFERRED: a GOV.UK service moves one page per step, so every outcome has a next page to land on. An app with long pages, background jobs and in-place edits does not. That is Kuiper's case.

### 1.4 Radix Toast (READ, MIT; `radix-ui/primitives`, `packages/react/toast/src/toast.tsx`, `main`)

- The viewport is `role="region"`, `tabIndex={-1}`, labelled "Notifications ({hotkey})", hotkey `F8`, around an `ol`. Focus proxies keep Tab order sane. When a focused toast closes, focus goes to the viewport.
- Each toast is an `li` with `tabIndex={0}` and no role.
- **Announcement** is a separate `ToastAnnounce`, portalled to `body`, visually hidden, `role="status"`, assertive for `foreground` and polite for `background`. Its text is written two animation frames late and removed after 1,000 ms.
- 5,000 ms default, paused on focus, pointer and window blur. Escape closes the focused toast. `Toast.Action` requires an `altText` naming another way to do the same thing.

### 1.5 Headless Svelte primitives (READ)

- **bits-ui has none**: 42 entries in `huntabyte-bits-ui/packages/bits-ui/src/lib/bits/`, no toast (EXECUTED, `ls`).
- **Melt** (`melt-ui-next-gen/packages/melt/src/lib/builders/Toaster.svelte.ts`): `closeDelay` 5,000 (line 101); the root is `popover: "manual"`, in the top layer (line 223); each toast `role: "alert"` with `aria-live` by type (317-321); pointer pauses it, focus does not (322-340).
- **Ark on `@zag-js/toast` 1.47.0** (`chakra-ui-ark/packages/svelte/src/lib/components/toast/`): a store with `max`, error-first priorities, `promise` and an Alt+T hotkey. Default durations: info and error 5 s, success 2 s, loading infinite (`https://zagjs.com/components/svelte/toast`).

### 1.6 `svelte-sonner`, what shadcn-svelte serves (EXECUTED, READ)

- `bun pm view`: 1.2.3, MIT, **0 dependencies** (it no longer brings `runed`), 101.56 KB unpacked, published 2026-11-02, one maintainer. A port of React `sonner` 2.0.8.
- The tarball, unpacked in the scratchpad: `dist/` is 204 KB; `Toast.svelte` and `Toaster.svelte` are 1,996 lines.
- Its DOM is as in 1.2.2 (overlays memo §4.1): `TOAST_LIFETIME = 4000`, three visible, 356 px wide, `hotkey = ['altKey','KeyT']`. The `section` is `aria-live="polite"`, and each `li` is `tabindex={0}` with its own `aria-live`, so live regions nest (`Toaster.svelte:387-393`, `Toast.svelte:520-533`).
- The hotkey handler never calls `preventDefault`. Escape collapses the list but dismisses nothing (`Toaster.svelte:245-262`). INFERRED: Option+T types † on macOS; Alt+T opens Firefox's Tools menu on Windows.
- shadcn-svelte's `sonner` item is two files over `svelte-sonner ^1.2.0` and `mode-watcher` (`shadcn-svelte/docs/static/registry/sonner.json`, `docs/package.json:141`).

## 2. What the accessibility rules require

- **SC 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable (A).** The Understanding page treats a message that "disappears after 5 seconds" as a time limit. Unless users can learn the same thing elsewhere, it must be possible to turn the limit off, adjust it tenfold, or extend it (READ, `https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html`). INFERRED: a timed toast passes if it repeats something shown elsewhere, or the app offers "keep notifications until I dismiss them" before it appears.
- **SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (A).** A spinner that runs past 5 s needs a way to stop it: dismiss, and reduced motion (INFERRED).
- **SC 4.1.3 Status Messages (AA).** Announce without focus: `status` for results, `alert` for errors (READ, overlays memo §4.2).
- **SC 1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus (AA)** is not strictly in play. Its spirit, content that stays while the pointer rests on it, argues for pausing on hover (INFERRED).
- **SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA)** names non-modal content as a risk. It passes if the user can reveal the focused item "without advancing the keyboard focus", such as with Escape (READ, `https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html`).
- **SC 2.1.1 Keyboard (A).** Close buttons and actions must be reachable. Sonner's are, but only after the whole page (overlays memo §4.1, EXECUTED).
- **Live regions across screen readers.** A region must exist before its text changes. Injecting a region with its text already in it is "consistently the least supported way" outside VoiceOver on macOS (READ, `https://www.scottohara.me/blog/2022/02/05/are-we-live.html`). `aria-atomic` support is inconsistent, and NVDA prefixes `role=alert` with "Alert" (READ, `https://www.sarasoueidan.com/blog/accessible-notifications-with-aria-live-regions-part-1/`). React Aria needed an `aria-hidden` flip for NVDA (§1.2). Radix warns assertive messages "may clear queued messages" (§1.4). Kuiper's `status` already renders empty from the first render (`src/lib/registry/ui/status/status.svelte`).
- **Route changes.** SvelteKit 3.1.0 speaks each new page's title through its own assertive `#svelte-announcer` (EXECUTED, `node_modules/@sveltejs/kit/src/runtime/components/root.svelte:8-53`). INFERRED: a toast spoken at the same instant may be cut off, so it should follow the title.
- **Modals.** With a bits-ui dialog or sheet open, `body` is `pointer-events: none` and a right sheet covers the corner (overlays memo §5.4, EXECUTED). Focus is trapped, so no hotkey reaches the toast. INFERRED, to be listened for: a live region outside an `aria-modal` dialog may go unspoken in some screen readers.

## 4. Recommendation: (b), `toast`, built on `status`

### 4.1 Semantics

- **Two announcers,** mounted with `<Toaster>` and server-rendered empty: Kuiper's `Status` (polite) and a sibling `role="alert"`. Errors go to `alert`, the rest to `status`.
- **Speaking.** One frame after a toast appears, its words go into an announcer: type word, title, description ("Error: Couldn't save. Check your connection."). They clear after about 1 s, so a repeat is spoken again. An action adds its reach: "… Undo. Press F8 to reach it", Radix's `altText` idea in our own form.
- **The visible stack is not live.** No nested regions; no "Dismiss" read aloud (INFERRED, §2).
- **Landmark.** A `section` labelled "Notifications", rendered only while it holds toasts, around an `ol`. An `li` is not a tab stop; its buttons are.
- **DOM position:** right after the skip link, so Tab from the top reaches it, as Roselli advises, matching the visual position below.
- **Keys.** F8 focuses the list (configurable). Escape there dismisses the focused toast. Leaving the list, or its last toast closing, returns focus to where it was. INFERRED: F8 is a media key on Mac laptops without fn, so the landmark and Tab are the other ways in. Alt+T is rejected as a typing chord.
- **Close:** Kuiper's `button` at icon size (`--size-action-small`, above 24 px), named "Dismiss", described by the title.
- **Layer:** `popover="manual"`, the top layer, as Melt does; no new `z-index`. The specification confirms support in the three engines (not checked here).

### 4.2 Rules

- **Kinds:** `default`, `success`, `info`, `caution`, `error`, `loading`, in `alert`'s tones. `info` waits on the tone inventory batch A's banner adds.
- **Timed:** `success`, `info` and `default` without an action, at `max(6000, 2000 + 250 × words)` ms, above Spectrum's 5 s floor.
- **Never timed:** `error`, `caution`, `loading`, and anything with an action. Every reference agrees (§1.1-1.2).
- **Pause,** keeping the remaining time: hover, focus in the region, `document.hidden`, window blur, an open modal. `<Toaster timers="off">` turns timers off; an app binds it to a user setting, SC 2.2.1's "turn off" (INFERRED).
- **Visible:** three at most, newest at the top as Carbon does. The rest queue with timers stopped. `error` jumps the queue (Zag's priorities). Persistent toasts sit below timed ones, our answer to Atlassian's no-mixing rule (INFERRED).
- **Placement:** top end, under the sticky bar when one is declared (`[data-sticky-bar]` → `--size-bar`, `019`'s declaration), 1rem in. Below 640 px, full width at the top, inside the safe area. INFERRED: the top stays clear of the chat composer and the on-screen keyboard.
- **Escape anywhere,** when no other layer handled it (`!event.defaultPrevented`), dismisses the newest dismissible toast, so a covered focus can be revealed (SC 2.4.11).
- **Modals:** a toast raised while one is open is held until it closes. A modal's own feedback belongs inside it (Primer, §1.3).
- **Route change:** a toast survives the navigation it was raised in (the "Project created" redirect, GOV.UK's next-page banner), spoken after SvelteKit's announcer. On the next navigation, timed and action toasts close; errors stay.
- **Promise:** `toast.promise(p, { loading, success, error })`. If `p` settles within 400 ms, no loading toast shows: no flash, no double speech. Otherwise the result replaces the loading toast in place and is spoken. Dismissing a loading toast hides it; the result still comes.
- **Dedupe:** an `id` updates in place. A repeat of a visible toast (same kind, title, description) refreshes it and is spoken again, without a second copy.
- **Server:** `toast()` does nothing on the server, so the module-level queue is never shared across requests (INFERRED).

### 4.3 API

```ts
toast(title, { description?, action?: { label, onclick }, id?, duration? }): string
toast.success | .info | .caution | .error(title, options)
toast.loading(title): string;  toast.update(id, patch);  toast.dismiss(id?)
toast.promise(promise, { loading, success: (v) => string, error: (e) => string })
```

```svelte
<Toaster label="Notifications" hotkey="F8" max={3} timers="on" />
```

- `toast.error` takes the `destructive` tone. `duration` can lengthen a timed toast, never shorten it below the floor or time an error.
- One `registry:ui` item, `toast`, with `registryDependencies` `status` and `button`. `app-shell` mounts `<Toaster>` after its skip link: an interface line in its changelog.

### 4.4 Tokens

- **Motion:** `--motion-slide` (220 ms) for enter and exit, `--motion-busy` for the spinner. Both are already 0 under reduced motion (`src/app.css:439-448`). No new motion token.
- **Elevation:** `shadow-elevation` (`app.css:326-328`), `popover`'s chrome.
- **New:** a width, `--size-toast` (about 22rem), and `info` if the banner has not added it. Timer values are behaviour, exported as constants, not tokens.

### 4.5 Tests that would prove it

- **Unit,** with fake timers: queue order, `max` and error priority; each kind's timer, and that `error`, `caution`, action and `loading` never expire; pause and resume keep the remaining time; `timers="off"`; a fast promise shows no loading toast; `id` updates; dedupe; the server no-op.
- **Browser,** with Playwright's `page.clock`:
  - Both announcers are in the server HTML, empty. After `toast.success`, `status` holds the expected words, then clears; after `toast.error`, `alert` does.
  - No `aria-live` inside the stack. No tabbable `li`. The close button is at least 24 × 24.
  - F8 focuses the list. Escape dismisses and returns focus to the element focused before.
  - Hover, `focusin`, `visibilitychange` and an open dialog each stop the expiry.
  - Under `emulateMedia({ reducedMotion: "reduce" })`, the toast's animations take 0 ms.
  - After a client navigation, the toast shows, and its words are written after `#svelte-announcer`'s.
  - With three toasts up, every Tab stop on a demo page is not entirely covered, or Escape reveals it.
  - axe in light and dark with every kind showing; a forced-colours screenshot.
- **The listen,** owner-run like `019`'s, as `scripts/a11y/voiceover-toast.ts`: each message spoken once and whole, an error interrupting, the action hint spoken, no button name spoken. An NVDA pass is worth having; NVDA is where React Aria found its quirk (§1.2).

## 5. Owner question

**Should success and info toasts time out?**

1. **Yes, after at least 6 s, longer for longer text.** They pause on hover, focus, a hidden tab and an open modal. An app-wide off switch is served for a user setting. Errors, cautions, actions and loading never time out. *(Recommended.)* It matches Spectrum, Atlassian and Carbon's actionable rule, and meets SC 2.2.1 through "turn off".
2. **No: every toast waits to be dismissed.** Carbon's code default and Roselli's position. The strictest reading of SC 2.2.1. Stacks fill, and every success costs a click.
3. **Sonner's defaults, 4 s for all.** Fails SC 2.2.1 without the switch. Listed for completeness.

Choosing (a) or (b) is the design lead's call unless the owner wants it put to them. This memo recommends (b).
