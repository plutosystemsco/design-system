# How definitive brands are built

- **Status:** complete
- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Author:** the design lead's researcher
- **Question:** How are "definitive" technology brands built? What few assets does each own, how are they protected, and how does the parent brand relate to its product family? What does that mean for Pluto Systems, a workspace suite on one governed data layer, whose only fixed constraint is "we need to compete with Google" and whose four-dot mark keeps a blue, a red, a yellow and a green?
- **What it blocks:** the identity option boards, and specification `009`.

## How to read this memo

Every claim carries a label.

- **EXECUTED:** we ran it and saw the result, on 2026-11-01.
- **READ:** a source says so. Every web source was read on 2026-11-01. Where only a search summary was seen, the claim says so.
- **INFERRED:** our reasoning.

Terms of art.

- **Distinctive asset.** A sign a reader links to one brand without its name: a shape, a colour set, a typeface, a sound.
- **Master brand.** The parent name that every product borrows trust from.
- **Brand architecture.** How a master brand and its products are named and shown together.
- **Branded house.** Every product carries the master name, as in "Google Maps".
- **Endorsed brand.** A product with its own name, plus a smaller parent credit, as in "Courtyard by Marriott".
- **House of brands.** Products with no visible parent, as with Procter & Gamble's Pampers.
- **Signature device.** A graphic element, other than the logo, that the brand owns and repeats, such as Mastercard's circles used alone.
- **Product icon.** The small square image that stands for one app on a home screen.
- **Variable font.** One font file whose weight, width or other qualities slide along **axes**. An **optical size** axis redraws letters for the size they are set at.
- **ΔE.** A number for how different two colours look. About 2.3 is the smallest difference most people notice.

## In short

- **Each definitive brand owns very few assets, and owns them hard** (READ, §1–§2). Google owns four tuned colours, a logotype, a G, four dots and a custom typeface. Microsoft owns four squares, four colours and Segoe. Each forbids others to use its colours or fonts.
- **Both giants drew their own typeface,** then grew it into a family (READ, §1.3, §2.2). Google Sans has an open licence since 2025. IBM and Figma did the same. IBM's reason was identity, plus about $1 million a year in licence fees.
- **The four-colour field is crowded** (READ, §6.1). Google, Microsoft, Slack, eBay and now Gemini all use blue, red, yellow and green.
- **Pluto's mark today sits close to two of them** (EXECUTED and INFERRED, §6.1). Its red is ΔE 2.7 from Google's red. Its yellow and green are within ΔE 7.5. Its reading order, blue, red, yellow, green, is the order in which Google's wordmark first shows each colour. Its structure, a tight 2×2 grid of four colours, is Microsoft's.
- **Giving every product icon all four colours failed** (READ, §1.4). Google's 2020 Workspace icons were called hard to tell apart, and Google has since moved toward one leading colour per app. Microsoft's 2018 Office icons gave each app its own colour from the start.
- **Both giants now run a branded house with an AI layer on top** (READ, §1.5, §2.3). Microsoft renamed the Office app "Microsoft 365", then "Microsoft 365 Copilot". Google made the gradient G its company-wide icon "in the AI era".
- **"Finished" is built from structure, not effects** (READ, §4): one claim, then proof as numbers and named customers, then product shown working.
- **Recommended for Pluto** (INFERRED, §6): own the *behaviour* of the four dots, not the colours. Retune the hues away from Google's. Commission a Pluto typeface with an open licence. Run a hybrid architecture: descriptive app names under "Pluto", and the data layer under its own open-source name, endorsed by Pluto.

## 1. Google

### 1.1 The 2015 identity: four parts

- Google's identity has a logotype, a compact G and the Google Dots. "The Dots are a dynamic distillation of the logotype for interactive, assistive, and transitional moments." They "indicate when Google is working for you" (READ, [Evolving the Google identity](https://design.google/library/evolving-google-identity/)).
- The brief asked for "dynamic, intelligent motion that responded to users at all stages of an interaction" (READ, same source).
- The colours were retuned: "We adjusted and pushed the vibrancy of the red, green, and yellow to maintain saturation and pop" (READ, same source).
- A special full-colour logo is "only 305 bytes, compared to our existing logo at ~14,000 bytes" (READ, same source).
- The G "uses increased visual weight to stand up at small sizes" (READ, same source).
- The dots' states included listening, thinking, replying, incomprehension and confirmation. They move "along geometric arcs" on "a standard set of snappy easing curves" (READ, [designboom, 2015](https://www.designboom.com/design/new-google-logo-09-01-2015/)).
- INFERRED: Google made one asset quiet (the G), one loud (the logotype) and one alive (the dots). Each has a job.

### 1.2 Colour rules

- Partners are told: "Don't use Google's brand colors in your work", covering sites, apps and merchandise. "Don't use or mimic Google's brand font in your work." "Only use the newest version of the Google G (the one with blended gradient colors)" (READ, [Google Partner Marketing Hub](https://partnermarketinghub.withgoogle.com/brands/google/branding-guidelines/how-to-show-googles-brand/)).
- EXECUTED: we searched for a published rule on colour *proportions*. Google publishes none we could find. The commonly quoted values are `#4285F4`, `#EA4335`, `#FBBC05` and `#34A853` (READ, a third-party brand kit, [brandkit.pro](https://brandkit.pro/brand/google)).

### 1.3 Typeface: from Product Sans to Google Sans Flex

- Product Sans came with the 2015 logo. It suited big product names, not interface text. Google Sans followed in 2018, with Colophon Foundry. Google Sans Text (2020) has "taller, more condensed letterforms" for small sizes. A mono, a code face and more than 20 writing systems followed (READ, [Making Google Sans Flex](https://design.google/library/google-sans-flex-font)).
- Google Sans Flex has six axes: weight, width, optical size, slant, grade and roundedness. Its designers call it "a power tool for expression". Google open-sourced Google Sans and Google Sans Flex in 2025 to narrow the gap between its apps and others' (READ, same source).
- Flex was released on 18 November 2025 under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (READ, [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/11/18/google-sans-flex-font-available/)).
- INFERRED: Google's face started as a logo lettering and earned its range from product needs. Open-sourcing it did not weaken the brand; it spread it.

### 1.4 Workspace icons, 2020 and after

- In October 2020 G Suite became Google Workspace. Gmail's envelope became an "M" built from all four colours. Calendar, Drive, Docs and Meet followed (READ, [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2020/10/06/new-gmail-icon/)).
- TechCrunch: "They all have all the colors, which just right off the bat makes it harder to tell them apart at a glance" (READ, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/06/googles-new-logos-are-bad)).
- A 2026 report says Google is preparing gradient app icons in which Gmail leans red and Calendar blue (READ, [Design Compass, 27 April 2026](https://designcompass.org/en/2026/04/27/google-apps-gradient-icon/); a leak, not an announcement).

### 1.5 The gradient G and Gemini

- The gradient G first stood for Search in May 2025. In September 2025 it became "the company-wide 'G' icon" and "now represents all of Google — both our brand and the company". The gradient is said to "symbolize the surge of AI-driven innovation". Google "brought this design to the Gemini spark in June" (READ, [Google blog, 29 September 2025](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/company-announcements/gradient-g-logo-design/)).
- Gemini's gradients have "sharp, almost opaque leading edges that diffuse at the tail". Its logo is "constructed from the negative space of four adjoining circles". The four dots were a reference for its "thinking state". "Movement in Gemini is not merely decorative; it's an essential guiding element" (READ, [Illustrating the Gemini app](https://design.google/library/gemini-ai-visual-design)).
- Material 3 Expressive, announced at I/O 2025, adds spring-based motion and a library of 35 shapes (READ, via search summary of [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-material-3-expressive-features-changes-availability-supported-devices-3556392/)).
- INFERRED: Google moved from flat blocks to blended light. Blended four-colour gradients are now Google's AI look.

## 2. Microsoft

### 2.1 The 2012 logo

- Unveiled 23 August 2012. "The symbol is important in a world of digital motion." "The symbol's squares of color are intended to express the company's diverse portfolio of products." "For the logotype, we are using the Segoe font which is the same font we use in our products" (READ, [Microsoft blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2012/08/23/microsoft-unveils-a-new-look/)).
- INFERRED: four equal squares are also a grid. Microsoft's tiles, icons and Copilot mark all return to the square.

### 2.2 Segoe

- Steve Matteson drew Segoe at Agfa Monotype around 2000. It replaced Franklin Gothic in branding and Tahoma in the interface. An EU design registration was revoked after Linotype argued it was too close to Frutiger (READ, [Wikipedia, Segoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segoe)).
- Segoe UI Variable has weight and optical-size axes. Optical size is automatic, favouring "legibility at small sizes and personality at large sizes" (READ, [Microsoft Learn, typography in Windows](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/signature-experiences/typography)).
- INFERRED: a custom face is a legal exposure as well as an asset. It must be original, not a near-copy.

### 2.3 The app icons and the master brand

- 2018: "Our design solution was to decouple the letter and the symbol." "Color differentiates apps and creates personality." Two panels add depth. The symbol now outweighs the letter, which used to take two-thirds of the icon (READ, [Redesigning the Office app icons](https://microsoft.design/articles/redesigning-the-office-app-icons-to-embrace-a-new-world-of-work/)).
- October 2022: the Office app was renamed the Microsoft 365 app. January 2025: renamed again to Microsoft 365 Copilot (READ, [Petri](https://petri.com/microsoft-365-app-rebrand-microsoft-365-copilot/); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/microsoft-365-copilot/the-microsoft-365-app-transition-to-the-microsoft-365-copilot-app)).
- October 2025: a refreshed icon set across the suite, Teams, Loop and Copilot (READ, [Redmond Magazine](https://redmondmag.com/Articles/2025/10/02/Microsoft-Rolls-Out-Copilot-and-New-Icons-Across-Office-Apps.aspx)).
- The new Copilot mark starts from "the square", making "two distinct forms that make one silhouette". "A shape in motion has to stay readable in every frame." Three gestures, Thinking, Planning and Retrieval, show when Copilot is working (READ, [More than a mark, 5 October 2026](https://microsoft.design/articles/more-than-a-mark/)).
- INFERRED: Microsoft retired "Office", a product brand, for the master brand, then attached the AI brand to it. App names stay plain: Word, Excel, Teams.

## 3. Workspace peers

- **Slack, 2019.** The old mark used 11 colours and had to sit at "the precisely prescribed 18º rotation" (READ, [Slack](https://slack.com/blog/news/say-hello-new-logo)). Pentagram cut it to red, yellow, green and blue, kept the octothorpe (`#`), and built it from pills and speech-bubble drops. Michael Bierut said the old brand was never "thought through as a system" (READ, [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/01/17/slack-logo-rebrand-pentagram/)).
- **Atlassian.** The 2017 rebrand gave 14-plus products one family: "individually unique but also part of a family", with a custom face, Charlie Sans (READ, [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/our-bold-new-brand)). A brand refresh launched at Team '24, with a UI refresh from October 2024 (READ, via search summary of [Atlassian developer community](https://community.developer.atlassian.com/t/a-visual-refresh-of-our-ui-foundations-is-coming/84949)). Loom's purple became blue "celebrating our place in the Atlassian product family" (READ, via search summary; the page refused our connection). Attribution logos are used "only … when the Atlassian context is not clear" (READ, [Atlassian Design](https://atlassian.design/foundations/logos/)).
- **Dropbox, 2017.** Collins reshaped the box into open platforms, chose Sharp Grotesk and paired clashing colours from "standard blue to 'whoa'". Critics called the colours jarring (READ, [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2017/10/11/dropbox-rebrand-logo-visual-identity-graphic-design/)). Today's standards define core, base and accent colours, each accent at 4.5:1 contrast (READ, via search summary of [Dropbox design standards](https://designstandards.dropbox.com/foundation/Color/intro)).
- **Notion.** "Our original branding and illustrations were all created in black and white," by Roman Muradov. A 2024 campaign added "bold yellows, blues, and reds" only so ads would stand out (READ, [Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/the-thinking-behind-our-latest-brand-campaign)).
- **Figma, 2024.** A custom Figma Sans by Grilli Type, plus condensed, mono and hand faces. Shape "primitives" have passive, activated and hyper-activated states. Maximal colour is "reserved for emphasis" (READ, [Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/figma-on-figma-evolving-our-visual-language/)).
- **Linear.** EXECUTED: our fetches of `linear.app` and `linear.app/brand` returned only "Loading…", so we could not read them. A secondary source says Linear moved headings to Inter Display in 2024 (READ, [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=114676)).
- INFERRED: the peers show three dials. Colour count (Slack cut, Dropbox expanded and was criticised). Quiet default with a loud mode (Notion, Figma). Parent-and-product family (Atlassian, Loom).

## 4. What "finished" is made of

Described structurally, from the pages as fetched on 2026-11-01.

- **Stripe** (READ, [stripe.com](https://stripe.com)). One claim: "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue." Then a customer-logo strip. Then six product tiles with a working billing mock-up. Then a proof band: "135+" currencies, "$1.9T" volume, "99.999%" uptime. Then enterprise, startup and platform stories, each with a number. Then API scale figures. Then news, a book, a closing call to action. Stripe's moving gradient is copied so often that clones exist on npm (READ, [stripe-gradient](https://npmjs.com/package/stripe-gradient)).
- **Vercel** (READ, [vercel.com](https://vercel.com)). "Agentic Infrastructure." Three sections, each with one named customer and one number, then four features. Vercel ships its own font family, Geist, under the OFL (READ, [Vercel](https://vercel.com/font)).
- **Cloudflare.** EXECUTED: our fetcher was served "a markdown version of the site for AI/LLM consumption", with products grouped and a numbered start guide.
- **Anthropic** (READ, [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com)). The proof is the public-benefit report, published system cards and dated model cards, not customer logos.
- INFERRED. "Finished" pages share six traits:
  1. one claim of under ten words;
  2. proof in three forms: a number, a named customer, a working product;
  3. a strict grid, with sections of one idea each;
  4. one owned visual device, used again and again;
  5. a custom or tightly limited type family;
  6. a version of the site for agents.

  The look itself (ribbon, glow, dark stage) is the part others copy, so it is the least defensible.

## 5. Signature devices and architecture

### 5.1 Devices that flex from quiet to loud

- **Mastercard** dropped its name in 2019, since "more than 80 percent of people" recognise the circles alone (READ, [Mastercard](https://newsroom.mastercard.com/news/press/2019/january/mastercard-evolves-its-brand-mark-by-dropping-its-name/)).
- **IBM.** Paul Rand drew the 8-bar logo in 1972 (READ, [IBM](https://www.ibm.com/history/logo)). IBM Plex (2017) replaced Helvetica to reflect IBM's character and save about $1 million a year (READ, [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/ibm-plex-typeface-graphic-design-141117)).
- **Google** dots animate as states (§1.1). **Microsoft** squares become grid and motion (§2.3). **Slack** keeps the `#` (§3). **Figma** states its primitives' loudness levels (§3).
- INFERRED: each device has a quiet form (mono, small, still) and a loud form (colour, large, moving). The device carries the brand where the logo is absent.

### 5.2 Brand architecture

- Aaker popularised "branded house" and "house of brands". Endorsed brands sit between them. Hybrids mix models across a portfolio (READ, [Wikipedia, Brand architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_architecture)).
- INFERRED. Suites converge on a branded house, because a suite sells integration. Google (Gmail, Docs, Meet) and Microsoft (Microsoft 365, Word, Teams) use plain app names under the master brand. Atlassian runs an endorsed family because its products arrived by acquisition. A house of brands suits unrelated markets, not one workspace.

## 6. Voice

- Google: "Focus on the user and all else will follow." "Fast is better than slow" (READ, [Google philosophy](https://about.google/philosophy/)).
- Microsoft's voice is "Warm and relaxed", "Crisp and clear", "Ready to lend a hand". "We write for scanning first, reading second" (READ, [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human)).
- Notion's first tagline: "Think it. Make it." (READ, §3). Stripe states what it is, plainly (READ, §4).
- INFERRED: "Work runs on data." already fits this pattern: one plain sentence, a claim, no adjectives. The house rule, short sentences with one idea each, is a voice principle in Microsoft's terms.

## 7. What it means for Pluto (INFERRED)

### 7.1 The derivative risk, measured

EXECUTED: we computed CIE76 ΔE between the mark tokens in `src/app.css` and the commonly quoted Google and eBay values.

| Dot | Pluto | vs Google | vs eBay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue | `#2456E6` | 29.9 | 23.0 |
| Red | `#E23A2E` | 2.7 | 6.5 |
| Yellow | `#F0AE12` | 7.5 | 3.2 |
| Green | `#1E9E56` | 7.3 | 41.9 |

INFERRED:

- The red is, to most eyes, Google's red. The yellow and green are near neighbours. Only the blue is clearly Pluto's.
- The reading order blue, red, yellow, green is the order Google's wordmark first shows each colour (G blue, o red, o yellow, l green).
- The mark's gap is 0.08 em on a 0.3 em dot (READ, `registry.json`). At small sizes four tight dots read as one tile, like Microsoft's.
- Together: Microsoft's structure, Google's sequence and Google's dots. A reader seeing a fragment may think "Google" before "Pluto". That is the opposite of definitive.

How to avoid it, without dropping the four colours:

1. **Retune the hues** to a family Google and Microsoft do not use. Suggestion: test deeper, inkier or more mineral versions. Hold each at ΔE ≥ 15 from Google, Microsoft, Slack and eBay. Make that a lint check.
2. **Reconsider the order.** Any order not starting blue-red-yellow breaks the Google echo. This is the owner's call; the mark's refinement is open.
3. **Open the gap.** A gap near one dot's width makes the mark read as `::`, punctuation, not a tile. The colon is a data character: a ratio, a key-value separator, a path. It ties to "Work runs on data."
4. **Default to mono.** Four colours appear only in the mark and in one device. Everything else is neutral plus one accent. Atlassian is said to keep its parent mark the only multicoloured one (READ, via search summary of [Designing Atlassian](https://medium.com/designing-atlassian/behind-the-scenes-of-the-atlassian-rebrand-f6ba1592377e)).
5. **Never blend the four.** Blended four-colour gradients are Google's AI look (§1.5). Pluto's colours stay discrete: dots, not light.
6. **Never use squares.** The square is Microsoft's (§2.3).
7. **Keep brand colour apart from status colour.** In an app, red, amber and green mean error, warning and success. Brand hues must be distinguishable from the status tokens, or never used where status is shown.

### 7.2 The few assets Pluto should own

Recognisable from a fragment means owning six things, each protected by lint where it can be:

1. the four-dot mark, retuned;
2. a dot system of devices (§7.3);
3. a Pluto typeface (§7.4);
4. a drawn wordmark (§7.5);
5. a colour rule: neutral ground, one accent, four colours only in the mark and device;
6. a voice: "Work runs on data." and its sentence style.

A motion signature belongs to the dot system, not to page chrome.

### 7.3 The mark as a system of devices

The mark is a 2×2 grid. Grids extend without redrawing. Each idea below uses the dots exactly as drawn.

- **The field.** The 2×2 tiles into an n×n field of mono dots, a map of a namespace. A few dots light in their brand colour where a search lands. Loud: a full-bleed stage of a field responding to a query. Quiet: a 4×4 field as a section marker.
- **The colon.** `::` as punctuation in type: "Work runs on data:", a path separator in product names, a bullet. Quietest form; works in plain text.
- **States.** The dots fill in reading order as an agent works: query, permission check, result. That echoes Google's dots in purpose but not in form, since Google's bounce and Pluto's would fill a grid. It also says the product's idea: one kernel checks every read.
- **Product glyphs.** Each product takes one dot position and one colour, plus a glyph. One leading colour per app follows Microsoft 2018 and avoids Google 2020.
- **Layout.** The dot pitch becomes a spacing unit, so pages are built on the mark's grid.

Loudness ladder: colon → mono mark → colour mark → field → animated stage. Each step is a component with its own lint profile.

### 7.4 Typeface options

1. **Recommended: commission a Pluto family,** sans and mono, variable with optical size. Precedent: Google Sans, Segoe, IBM Plex, Charlie Sans, Figma Sans. Release it under the OFL, as Google and IBM did. That fits a public-benefit corporation and "source, not a package". Risk: cost and time, and it must be original (§2.2).
2. **Interim: one open family, used strictly.** Plex, Geist and JetBrains Mono are IBM's, Vercel's and JetBrains' signatures. Three borrowed faces make a fragment read as someone else. Pick one superfamily with a mono. Plex has sans, serif and mono; Geist has sans, mono and pixel.
3. **Derive from an OFL face.** The OFL permits modified versions under a new name; each font's Reserved Font Name must be checked first. Cheaper than option 1, less owned.
4. **Keep today's three faces.** Cheapest; least definitive.

### 7.5 Wordmark directions

1. **Recommended: a drawn lowercase "pluto"** from the Pluto face's display cut, spaced for the `::` mark to sit as a leading colon.
2. **A mono-derived wordmark,** which signals data and code. Risks reading as a developer tool, not a workspace.
3. **A heavier geometric wordmark.** Risks Google Sans, which is geometric and rounded.
4. **Dots inside the letters,** such as a dotted "o". Gimmicky, and two dot signs compete.

### 7.6 Architecture options for a suite plus a data platform

The `v0.8.0` decision gives each product its own name and logo, with Pluto's endorsement off by default (READ, `decisions.md`). That is an endorsed or house-of-brands model. The owner's goal points elsewhere.

1. **Recommended: hybrid.** Apps take plain names under the master brand: Pluto Mail, Pluto Docs, Pluto Sheets. The data layer keeps its open-source name, vfs, endorsed "by Pluto". *Pro:* the suite builds one name, as Google and Microsoft do. The open-source project keeps a neutral name contributors can adopt. *Con:* two names to grow; vfs is generic and hard to protect.
2. **Pure branded house.** Pluto Mail … and Pluto Data or Pluto Kernel. *Pro:* strongest single brand; simplest. *Con:* the open-source layer reads as a vendor product, which may slow outside adoption.
3. **Endorsed family** (today's model). Each product named on its own, "A Pluto Systems product" when context is unclear. *Pro:* products can be sold or spun out. *Con:* every name starts from zero; the suite's integration story is weaker.
4. **Master brand plus agent brand.** Pluto, plus a name for the agent layer, as Copilot and Gemini sit over their suites. *Pro:* matches how both giants now market AI. *Con:* premature before the suite exists.
5. **House of brands.** Not recommended for one integrated workspace.
