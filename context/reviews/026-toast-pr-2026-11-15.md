## Findings, most severe first

1. **High. A focused container hides every toast, and the count line, until focus moves.** (`toaster.svelte`, `checkAside`, lines 256 to 283)
   - **The defect.** A toast steps aside when its box overlaps the focused element's box at all (decision 15a). A container that takes focus by script, such as `<main id="main" tabindex="-1">` after a skip link, is as wide as the page. Every toast overlaps it. So every toast steps aside. The count line overlaps it too, so it steps aside as well. Nothing is left on screen.
   - **Evidence.** EXECUTED, `review-026/scripts/big.ts`: a success raised by keyboard, then `main` focused by script, as a skip link does. In Chromium, Firefox and WebKit alike: `main` 1104 × 12,445 px, `:focus-visible` true, 1 toast aside, the count line reads "1 hidden" but is itself hidden, and the success is still there after 7 s, its clock stopped.
   - **It already fails a test.** WebKit, test 57 "an open dialog": Safari does not focus a clicked button, so when the alert dialog closes, focus lands on the page's container (`review-026/scripts/wk.ts`: `active` "Components Feedback Toast…", `aside` 1). The toast never leaves.
   - **Why it matters.** The app shell's skip link targets exactly such a `#main` (`+layout.svelte`, `<main id="main" tabindex="-1">`), and Q6a puts the toaster right after that skip link. After D1, a keyboard user who skips to content loses every toast, timed ones included, until focus moves. SC 2.4.11 does not ask for this: a toast cannot hide a container larger than itself.
   - **Fix (a small change in the item):** step aside only when the toast could hide the focused element. For example, skip the check when the focused element's box is wider than the region or taller than the viewport; or, for an element larger than a toast, use the criterion's own test, "entirely hidden". Never hide the count line for such an element. Add a test: Tab to a skip link, Enter, focus on `#main`: every toast visible and timing. Re-run test 57 in WebKit.

2. **High, trivial. `bun run check` fails.** (`tests/e2e/toast.spec.ts:479`, `:481`)
   - **The defect.** `hold: async (page) => page.evaluate(() => window.dispatchEvent(new Event("blur")))` returns `Promise<boolean>`, typed `Promise<void>`. The same for `"focus"`. svelte-check: "Type 'Promise<boolean>' is not assignable to type 'Promise<void>'", twice.
   - **Why it matters.** Criterion 27 and the CI gate. The Landing notes' "Checks run" says 0 errors.
   - **Fix:** a block body, `async (page) => { await page.evaluate(…); }`, for both. Re-run `bun run check` and correct the Landing notes' line.

3. **Medium. One `held` flag stands for two holds, so a pointer's leave lets a focused toast be displaced.** (`toast-queue.ts`, `held`, line 119, and `hold`, lines 699 to 710; `toaster.svelte`, lines 107 to 120 and 550 to 551)
   - **The defect.** Focus and the pointer both call `queue.hold(id, on)`, which sets one boolean. Focus in a toast, then the pointer passing over it and out, sets `held` to false while focus is still there. `syncFocus` does not set it again, since the focused id has not changed (line 116).
   - **Evidence.** EXECUTED, unit scenario: `max` 1; A held by focus, held and released by the pointer; an error raised: showing is the error alone. EXECUTED, browser, 390 × 844 (`review-026/scripts/live.ts`): "Save", F8, Tab to its Dismiss; the mouse over the toast and away; "Save with an error" dispatched. Result: `{"kinds":["error"],"focusIn":"error","more":"1 more"}`. The control, without the hover: the success stays, focus stays. Screenshot `shots/rc-390-after.png`.
   - **Why it matters.** Decision 6: "A toast that holds focus or the pointer never leaves the stack except by closing." Focus jumps to a toast the person did not choose. Test 53 passes only because it never moves the mouse.
   - **Fix:** two flags, `focused` and `pointed`, with `held = focused || pointed`; `hold(id, cause, on)`. Add the hover-out step to test 10 and a test 53b.

4. **Medium. A success raised with two errors may never be heard; three schedule rules are untested.** (`toast-queue.ts`, `write`, lines 451 to 473)
   - **The defect.** `write()` writes the polite message first, then any assertive one in the same frame. For a success and two errors in one task, the trace is (EXECUTED, unit scenario R-b): success and error 1 at 16 ms; both clear at 1,016; at 1,032 the success's rewrite **and** error 2 are written in the same frame; at 2,032 both clear. The rewrite is interrupted at once, and the rewrite-once guard stops a second. Decision 10's purpose, "an error does not silence a success raised beside it", fails for two errors.
   - **Untested rules.** Mutants 8, 9 and 10 survive the whole unit file: the rewrite-once guard, "while an assertive message is held, polite writes wait" (test 26's late success waits behind the polite clear, not the assertive one, so it cannot tell), and a waiting toast's update being spoken when it shows.
   - **Fix:** a polite write, or at least a rewrite, waits while the assertive channel has an unblocked message queued. Add unit tests: an error alone, then a success at 500 ms, written after the error clears; a success with two errors, heard once after the last; a displaced toast updated while waiting, spoken when it returns. If the owner would rather wait for `022`'s readers, record this as a known gap in the Landing notes and add the two-error case to S10.

5. **Medium (process). Criterion 28 is not met as written, and the deviation is a rule's, not only the spec's.**

   - **Fix:** in the Landing notes, record it under a "Checkpoint rulings" line for the design lead to accept, and restate criterion 28 as met for the unit tests and proven by mutation for the browser tests.
