## Findings, most severe first

1. **The Escape answer to SC 2.4.11 does not hold for toasts** (decision 15, "Escape outside the region answers SC 2.4.11"; Q4; tests 51 and 52; decision 22's "Escape reveals a covered focus"; decision 26's changelog).
   - The Understanding page's Note 2 reads: "Content opened by the user may obscure the component receiving focus. If the user can reveal the focused component without advancing the keyboard focus…" The section on persistent content then says: "This section only applies to content that the user actively discloses. Content pre-positioned by the author (such as a sticky footer), or content that appears without direct user initiation, such as system warnings, must not prevent the item receiving focus from being immediately visible in the viewport" (READ, `w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html`).
   - A toast is not content the user discloses. The person asked to save, not for a toast. The spec's own intent names "a job finished in the background", which appears with no user action at all. The memo cites the same page (§2) but not this limit.
   - Failure: a background toast appears at the top end while an action button sits under it (on the docs page, or in a content area's top-right toolbar). The person Tabs to that button, and it is entirely covered. Escape would reveal it, but the criterion does not accept Escape for this kind of content. Test 52 passes ("or Escape reveals it") on a page that fails the criterion.
   - Fix:
     - The stack must never entirely cover the focused element. On `focusin` outside the region, if any showing toast's box covers the focused element's box, those toasts step aside until focus moves elsewhere. Stepping aside means the stack moves to the bottom end, or the covering toasts are hidden with `visibility: hidden`, kept in the queue and paused. They are not dismissed, and they are not made translucent (decision 8).
     - Test 52 drops "or Escape reveals it". Test 51 keeps Escape as an extra, not as the criterion's evidence.
     - Q4 is re-asked with the new options. Recommended: (1) covering toasts step aside while focus is under them, and Escape outside closes nothing. Then (2) step aside, and Escape also closes the covering toasts. Then (3) the stack moves to the bottom end while focus is under it.
     - The page's "Provides" bullet and the changelog stop saying Escape is the answer.

2. **De-duplication by content merges distinct action toasts** (decision 9, "By content"; test 13; decision 22's `action` example, whose `onclose` "commits the delete unless the reason is `"action"`").
   - The match is kind, title and description. The action is not compared, and the second call's `action` and `onclose` have no stated fate.
   - Failure: delete message A, which raises "Message deleted" with Undo(A) and `onclose` committing A. Then delete message B. Same kind, same text, no `id`, so no new toast. Either B's Undo is dropped, and Undo restores A while B is deleted with no undo. Or B's `onclose` never runs, so B's delete is never committed. Both are wrong answers for the reference pattern the spec itself ships.
   - The same gap is in "By `id`": an update "replaces its kind, title, description and action", but it does not say which `onclose` runs. "`onclose` runs once per toast" does not settle it.
   - Fix:
     - De-duplicate by content only when neither toast has an `action` or an `onclose`. Otherwise raise a new toast.
     - For an update by `id`, state the rule. Recommended: the new `onclose` replaces the old one, and the old one runs once with a new reason, `"replaced"`. Or the old one is kept and the new one ignored, with a development warning.
     - Add unit tests: two action toasts with equal text give two toasts and two `onclose` calls; an `id` update's `onclose` rule.

3. **The info and loading edge is translucent** (decision 13, "`info` and `loading`: `text-muted-foreground`, and the hairline edge"; decision 8; test 53).
   - The hairline in this system is `border-border`, `--border: #0b0f171a` in light and `#f3f0e81a` in dark (READ, `src/app.css:27`, `:209`). That is 10% alpha. `019a`'s landing notes found exactly this hairline breaking the depth rule in the bar ("the 10% hairline (`rgba(11, 15, 23, 0.1)` …)"). `alert`'s `caution` variant, which decision 13 says it draws like, uses `border-border` for its other sides (READ, `alert.svelte`).
   - Failure: a builder follows "the hairline edge" or `alert`'s classes. Test 53 then finds a border alpha of 0.1 on every info and loading toast, and the build fails on its own specification. Or the builder improvises.
   - Fix: name the classes. For example, every side is `border-transparent` 1 px (decision 8), and the start edge is `--focus-width` in the kind's colour, or `border-s-input` (solid `#857e70` / `#7a7366`) for info and loading. The visible outline is `shadow-elevation`'s solid `--elevation-line` ring (READ, `app.css:58`, `:326–328`). Say that test 53 reads borders, so a box-shadow ring is allowed.

4. **Focus can be lost when a toast leaves for a reason other than closing** (decision 6, "an error does not wait behind a timed toast"; decision 6's `max` of 1 below 640 px; decision 17; decision 15's "Focus moves on" rule, which covers only "when the toast holding focus closes").
   - A timed toast with focus is paused, but it is still timed. The displacement rule picks "the oldest showing timed toast", which can be the focused one. On a phone, with `max` 1, it is always the focused one.
   - Failure: at 390 px, F8, Tab to a success toast's Dismiss. A background error arrives, the success returns to the queue, and its `li` leaves the DOM. Focus falls to `body`, and a screen-reader user is thrown to the top of the page.
   - The same happens if the window narrows below 640 px while focus is in the second toast (`setMax`). It also happens on a navigation that closes a focused toast, where the rule's "same control in the toast above" is undefined.
   - Fix: displacement skips a toast that holds focus or the pointer; if every showing timed toast does, the error waits at the head. The "Focus moves on" rule covers every way a toast leaves the list: close, displacement, `setMax`, navigation and unmount. Add a unit test and a browser test at 390 px.

5. **The two announcers are not coordinated** (decision 10, "The schedule, per announcer").
   - The memo cites Radix: assertive messages "may clear queued messages" (§1.4, §2). Each announcer's schedule is independent, so an error raised within a second of a success writes both at once. On NVDA, the interruption can flush the queued polite speech.
   - Failure: a save that succeeds on one record and fails on another raises `toast.success` and `toast.error` in one task. The person hears only the error. The visible stack shows both, and nothing says a success was missed.
   - Fix: one schedule across both channels. While an assertive message is being held (its 1,000 ms), polite writes wait. A polite message written less than 1,000 ms before an assertive one is written again after the assertive one clears. Add unit test "an error does not silence a success raised beside it" and an S-test that checks both phrases are spoken.

6. **A non-finite or huge `duration` closes the toast at once** (decision 5, "`max(computed, duration)`"; decision 26's guide, "`duration` below 6000 is raised to 6000").
   - `Math.max(6000, Infinity)` is `Infinity`, and `setTimeout(fn, Infinity)` fires after about 0 ms, because the delay is converted to a 32-bit integer. `Math.max(6000, NaN)` is `NaN`, which also fires at 0. A value above 2,147,483,647 overflows the same way.
   - Failure: an app moving from `sonner` keeps `toast.success("Saved", { duration: Infinity })`, which is `sonner`'s documented way to keep a toast. In Kuiper the toast flashes and closes, with reason `"timeout"`.
   - Fix: a non-finite `duration`, or one above 2³¹ − 1, makes the toast persistent. A `NaN` or negative value is ignored, with a development warning. The guide says "`duration: Infinity` keeps the toast, as in `sonner`". Test 2 gains these cases.

7. **The mutation's expected set cannot be predicted** (decision 20, "One mutation joins `tests/sr/mutations/`"; `022` decision 15: the report "fails unless they match exactly … and no other").
   - The region, and so the `ol`, is rendered only while it holds a toast (decision 12, part 1; the outline's `{#if snapshot.showing.length}`). S3 raises one toast on a fresh page, so the mutated `aria-live` list is inserted with its content already in it. O'Hara calls that "consistently the least supported way" outside VoiceOver on macOS (memo §2). Under NVDA the list may say nothing at all, and S3 passes.
   - Where the list is spoken, it repeats the announcer's message. That breaks `022`'s `live` rule ("It is not read twice in a row") in S1, S2 and S4 as well, so more than S3 fails.
   - Failure: the mutation run reports a mismatch against `expected.json` either way, and criterion 20 cannot be met without guessing.
   - Fix:
     - The patch also renders the region and the list empty from the first render, so the live list exists before its text changes.
     - `expected.json` names every S-test whose action raises a toast while the list is live (S1 to S4, S7 and S8 at least), or the mutation is narrowed so only S3's `unspoken` clause can catch it.
     - Or the mutation is chosen to touch one rule only, and the "fault `005` named" is proven by a unit-level DOM check instead (test 39 already does).

8. **`app-shell` would declare `live` with no test, and the server path is never listened to** (decision 20, "`app-shell`'s entry gains `live`"; test 64; `022` test 5: "each `SR_ITEMS` entry has a `tests/sr/<item>.sr.ts` with at least one test per declared kind").
   - Every S-test runs on `/example/toast/…`, whose toaster decision 24 loads lazily, on the client. So no S-test hears the announcers the shell renders on the server. That is the path decision 18 and test 36 exist for, and the one most apps will run.
   - Failure: test 64 fails, since `app-shell.sr.ts` has no `live` test. Or the builder drops `live` from `app-shell`, and the shell's toast speech is never proven by a screen reader.
   - Fix: add an S-test to `tests/sr/app-shell.sr.ts`. On a shell view, a button raises `toast.success`, and the rule is `live`. This needs a trigger in the view, so name the view file. Or render the example view's toaster on the server for `item === "toast"` (a static import behind the route's `load`), so the S-tests hear the server-rendered path.

9. **Releasing held toasts as a modal closes falls on VoiceOver's known drop** (decision 16, "When the last modal closes, the waiting toasts show and are spoken"; decision 10, "a message is written one frame after its toast shows").
   - WebKit bug 222190 reports VoiceOver on macOS skipping live-region announcements "when they coincide with a view or focus change". The reporter's example is a "Modal has been closed" message lost on Escape or the close button. It was reproduced on macOS 13.1, and moved to Apple's own tracker unfixed (READ, `bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=222190`).
   - Closing a modal returns focus to the trigger at the same moment the hold is released. That is S9's exact case.
   - The hold itself is well supported. VoiceOver ignores live regions outside an `aria-modal` dialog, and [w3c/aria#1854](https://github.com/w3c/aria/issues/1854), open, records it, with JAWS, NVDA and Narrator still reading them. Q7's option 1 is right, and the evidence can be READ, not INFERRED.
   - Fix: after the last modal closes, polite and assertive writes wait as they do after `navigated()`. That is `TOAST_ROUTE_HOLD_MS`, or a named `TOAST_MODAL_HOLD_MS` that the run tunes. Unit test 20 checks the delay. Cite the two sources in decision 16 and the Risks.

10. **SC 2.2.1 is met by the app, not by the item alone** (decision 5; decision 22, "Provides … `timers`" and "Your part: … bind `timers` to a setting for people who need more time"; decision 23, where the shell mounts `<Toaster />` with timers on).
    - Understanding 2.2.1 accepts a disappearing message when "Users are able to identify the arrival of email through other means". Otherwise "each message would need to meet this success criterion". Turning off must be possible "before encountering it" (READ, `w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html`). Pausing on hover and focus is not one of the criterion's three options.
    - The shell installs timed toasts with no setting. A default Kuiper app conforms only if every timed toast repeats something on the page. Constitution §4.4 says every item meets AA by default, and that a gap is "a finding to fix, never as a limit to record".
    - Fix:
      - State the condition where the page and the changelog claim the behaviour: timed toasts meet 2.2.1 when the message is also on the page, or when the app binds `timers` to a setting the person can reach first.
      - Make "Your part" a must, not advice.
      - Make the `result` pair's "do" carry the rule ("the result is also on the page").
      - Have the shell's comment block name the setting. Optionally the `settings` block gains the switch in a later spec.
      - This is the owner's ruling 5, so only the wording changes, not the default.

11. **The cost case does not fit `025`** (decision 25; criterion 26; `025` decisions 2, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10).
    - **The fixture.** `025` decision 8: one file over `src/routes/+page.svelte`, importing "only from its own item's installed path, and from `svelte`". The root layout is the cost app's, with the `cost:hydrated` mark. "A root layout with `<Toaster />`" is not a fixture `025` can hold. Mount the toaster in the page fixture.
    - **The interaction.** It needs an `interactions.ts` entry (`025` criterion 2): kind `click`, a `data-cost="target"`, an expected state (for example `[data-slot=toast]` present and `toaster-status` non-empty), and a `says` verb such as "shows". "Measured as INP is" should read "`025`'s response time (decision 6)".
    - **The large cases.** `025` cases budget hydration and response time only. "The task's longest frame", "no long task over 50 ms" and "nothing spoken after the first" are metrics `025` does not have. Restate each as an interaction whose response time is budgeted (a click that raises 100 toasts; a click that starts 200 updates, ending when the last is drawn). Move the speech clause to the unit suite, which test 12 already covers.
    - **The `sonner` reference.** `025` decision 2's cost app lists "every npm package any item names", pinned by its own `bun.lock`, and installs from `static/r/` only. `svelte-sonner` and `mode-watcher` are named by no Kuiper item. Measuring them adds two packages to a committed lockfile, against the header's "No package is added to the repository". Either drop the figure, or state the exception and pin it, and ask the owner.
    - **The estimates.** `025`'s alone figure counts `cn` (10.9 kB gzipped) and every dependency. `button` alone hydrates 31 ms over the floor, and figures under 5 ms show as "under 5 ms" (`025` "What the research found", decision 5). "4 to 7 kB of JS" and "under 1 ms" are not what the harness will report. Give the estimate as `toast`'s `own` group, or as the alone figure above `button`'s.

12. **Closing action and caution toasts on the second navigation is a hidden owner question** (decision 17, "every toast that has already survived one closes … unless it is an error or loading").
    - Ruling 6 says actions "never time out". A navigation close is not a time-out. It still ends an Undo window without the person's act, and the `action` example's `onclose` then commits the delete.
    - Ask it as Q8: (1) action and caution toasts stay until dismissed, like errors (recommended, closest to ruling 6); (2) they close on the second navigation, as drafted.

13. **`afterNavigate` runs at hydration** (decisions 17 and 18; READ, `root.svelte:11–18`, where the first `afterNavigate` only sets `mounted`).
    - The shell's `afterNavigate(() => toast.navigated())` also runs once at hydration, with no `beforeNavigate` before it.
    - Failure: a page raises "Welcome back" in `onMount`. Decision 18 keeps it for the toaster. The hydration `navigated()` marks it survived, so the first real navigation closes it, contrary to "A toast survives one navigation".
    - Fix: `navigated()` without a pending `navigating()` does nothing. Unit test 18 gains the case.

14. **Two tests are vacuous** (test 22; test 32).
    - Test 22's "a later `createToastQueue` snapshot is empty" is true of any fresh queue. Check instead that, after the server-side call, setting a fake `document` and calling `toast.success` again yields a queue holding one toast, not two.
    - Test 32 ("0 literals and 0 allowances") passes before the item exists. It must also assert that the lint's file list includes the six toast files. `literals.test.ts` pins a `FILE_COUNT` (line 37), so say that count moves by 6.

15. **A `Toast` specimen is an `li` outside a list** (decision 12, part 3; decision 22's `kinds` example and the pairs).
    - Five specimens in an `inert` wrapper, and the "do" specimens, which are not inert, render `li` elements under a `div` or inside the page's `dos` list item. axe's `listitem` rule reports them wherever they are exposed. The live "do" specimens' Dismiss and Undo are page tab stops that do nothing.
    - Fix: specimens are wrapped in an `ol` (the `kinds` example, and each pair file). Every specimen, "do" or "don't", is rendered `inert`, so the docs page gains no dead stops. Or `Toast` takes `as="div"` for specimens.

16. **Forced colours keep the edge but not its colour** (decision 14, "The edge colours are border colours, so they stay").
    - Under `forced-colors: active` the border colour is forced to a system colour. The transparent border becomes visible, which is the intent, but the start edge loses its kind colour. The kind is then carried by the icon's shape alone.
    - That is acceptable: `Info`, `CheckCircle`, `Warning` and `WarningCircle` differ in shape, and the hidden kind word speaks it. Say so, and have criterion 22 or test 56 check that each kind's icon differs.

17. **Placement write-ins** (decision 7; test 49).

## The specification's claims, checked

- **bits-ui reads a top-layer click as outside a modal.** It holds, with one correction. The dismissable layer listens for `pointerdown` on `document`. It treats the event as outside when the target is not inside the content and the point lies outside the content's box (READ, `use-dismissable-layer.svelte.js:247–263`, `dom.js:16–20`). A toast in the top layer is still a DOM child of `body`, so it inherits `pointer-events: none` (READ, `body-scroll-lock.svelte.js:185`). Unless it sets `pointer-events: auto`, the click falls through to the scrim, which is outside too. The modal closes either way. ### Remaining findings

**R1. Medium. The step-aside check misses scrolls inside panels and layout shifts** (decision 15a's triggers: `focusin`, a stack change, `resize`, `scroll`).
- `scroll` does not bubble. A listener on `window` never hears an `overflow: auto` panel scrolling, such as the sidebar inset, a code block or a table.
- No trigger fires when content above the focused element grows and pushes it under the stack. A late image, an expanding accordion, or streamed chat text all do this.
- Failure: the focused element moves under a visible toast with no `focusin`, so it is covered until the next trigger.
- Fix:
  - listen for `scroll` in the capture phase on `document`;
  - add a `ResizeObserver` on `document.documentElement`;
  - or run the check once a frame while a toast shows and focus is outside the region (a few `getBoundingClientRect` calls).
  - Test 64 gains two cases: a panel scrolled under the stack, and content inserted above the focused button.

**R2. Medium. The step aside hides the result of a top-end action, and Q4 does not say so** (decision 15a, "A toast that first shows while focus is under it shows stepped aside"; Q4 option 1; the Risks).
- Chromium and Firefox focus a button on click. A "Save" button at the top end of a toolbar, directly under the stack, keeps focus after it is pressed.
- Failure: its success toast arrives stepped aside. A sighted person never sees it while focus stays on "Save". The "more" line excludes aside toasts, so nothing shows that a toast exists. A mouse user is hit hardest: SC 2.4.11 concerns keyboard focus, and they gain nothing from the hiding.
- Fix:
  - Step aside only when the focused element matches `:focus-visible`, so a pointer press never hides the toast it raised.
  - State the remaining keyboard case in Q4 and the Risks: Enter on a top-end "Save" shows nothing until focus moves.
  - Let the owner weigh option 1 against option 3, the stack moving to the bottom end, which hides nothing.
  - Optionally, the "more" line also counts aside toasts ("1 hidden").
